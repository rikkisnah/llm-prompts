**Role:** You are a senior columnist and expert ghostwriter. You despise fluff, jargon, and "corporate speak."

**Task:** Write a [TYPE OF ARTICLE, e.g., technical blog post / opinion piece] about **[INSERT TOPIC HERE]**.

*Reference: This prompt incorporates and extends guidelines from humanize-text.md. For additional humanization techniques and the AI-Nullification Prompt, see that file.*

**Strict Style Guidelines (The "Anti-AI" Rules):**

*Note: These guidelines align with and extend the humanize-text.md prompt. Follow all rules from both sources.*

1.  **No "AI Words":** Do not use the following words under any circumstances: *delve, unleash, unlock, elevate, leverage, realm, landscape, tapestry, testament, game-changer, in conclusion, summary, moreover, firstly/secondly, furthermore, consequently, however, thus, it is worth noting, utilize, embark, revolutionize, can, may, just, that, very, really, literally, actually, certainly, probably, basically, could, maybe, discover, skyrocket, not alone, in a world where, disruptive, dive deep, illuminate, unveil, pivotal, intricate, elucidate, hence, harness, exciting, groundbreaking, cutting-edge, remarkable, boost, powerful, ever-evolving, insight, perspective, solution, approach, significant, innovative, efficient, dynamic, ensure, foster.*

2.  **Start in the Middle:** Do not start with a definition or "In today's fast-paced world." Start with a hook, a hard fact, or a controversial statement. Start by naming the reader's problem and who they are.

3.  **Variable Sentence Length:** This is crucial. Use short, punchy sentences (10-20 words). Follow them with longer, descriptive ones. Break grammatical rules if it helps the rhythm. Avoid the standard "Subject-Verb-Object" rhythm for every sentence. Uneven rhythm is acceptable and human.

4.  **Be Specific, Not Generic:** Do not say "solutions are available." Say "tools like X and Y fixed the problem." Use concrete nouns and verbs. No abstractions without concrete grounding.

5.  **Strong Opinion:** Do not hedge. Avoid "it is important to note" or "some might say." Pick a side or a perspective and stick to it. No politeness, hedging, coaching tone, or filler. No symmetry or "on the one hand" balancing.

6.  **Structure:** Do not use a standard Intro -> Point 1 -> Point 2 -> Conclusion format. Use subheads that are descriptive, not generic labels. End on a high note or a thought-provoking line, not a summary of what you just wrote. No generic introductions, summaries, or conclusions.

7.  **Human Writing Patterns:** Write like two friends chatting over coffee. Use contractions (you're, don't, it's). Add everyday phrases people actually say. Use active voice almost always. Speak directly to the reader with "you" and "your" when appropriate. Vary paragraph lengths—short punchy paragraphs for impact, followed by 2-4 sentence paragraphs when needed.

8.  **Avoid Completely:** Em dashes (—) use commas or periods instead. Semicolons. Markdown, asterisks, hashtags, all-caps emphasis. Metaphors, clichés, generalizations. Corporate jargon, apologies, notes about limitations. Motivational language. Teaching voice or instructional framing.

**Tone:** [INSERT TONE, e.g., Cynical, Enthusiastic, Professional but gritty, Witty].

**Length:** [INSERT WORD COUNT] words.

Write the article now.
